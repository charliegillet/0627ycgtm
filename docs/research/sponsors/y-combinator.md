## What YC values in a demo / pitch

From YC's own *Guide to Demo Day Presentations* and Startup School:

- **Clarity above all.** "Clearly and simply presented." If a slide has more than ~7
  words "it is likely to be too many." Avoid jargon and "marketing boilerplate."
- **Show traction / real usage, not promises.** "Bring out your hockey sticks and
  show us your startup growth curves" to prove the story is "non-fiction." Lead with
  metrics; don't bury them.
- **Make the opportunity concrete.** Size markets "bottoms up" (price x customer
  count), in **dollars not percentages**, not vague trillion-dollar claims.
- **The product is the proof.** Synthesize "especially, the product you are
  building." A working product that does the thing beats a description of one.
- **Caveat for *our* context:** YC's Demo Day guide warns "Demos seldom work in
  modern demo day presentations" and videos are "almost never a good idea" - but that
  advice is about *investor* Demo Day with 60-second slots and flaky live wifi. This
  hackathon explicitly wants a **working demo + 3-min video and discourages slides**,
  so we should optimize for a crisp, *recorded* product walkthrough that obviously
  runs on real data. The YC principle still holds: show the thing working on a real
  signal, don't narrate.

**Translation for our 3-min video:** open on the working product processing a real,
specific signal; show the output a salesperson would actually act on; quantify the
value ("this surfaces N ready-to-buy accounts/day a rep would have missed"). No
slides, minimal talk, let the product carry it.

## YC's POV on AI GTM, agents, and distribution moats

### Distribution / GTM is the founder's job, and it's the bottleneck
YC's foundational text is Paul Graham's *Do Things That Don't Scale* (2013): "The most
common unscalable thing founders have to do at the start is to recruit users
manually." Canonical examples (Stripe's "Collison installation," Airbnb door-to-door,
DoorDash flyers) are all *manual GTM*. Garry Tan (YC President/CEO) reinforces: the
limiting factor is no longer technical ability but "whether founders can get in the
heads of customers." YC's most popular internal event is reportedly the **sales
mini-conference** - sales mindset is treated as core, not optional.

### Evals / custom insight = the real moat (directly on-thesis)
Garry Tan, **Feb 21, 2025** (X): *"Evals are emerging as the real moat for AI startups.
Hard won insights about customers and their business logic discovered by founders
acting almost as ethnographers spelunking in the underserved slices of the GDP pie
chart."* This is the single most useful quote for our pitch - it is the YC-sanctioned
version of "the higher-leverage signals are custom, not bought." A custom signal +
custom eval/scoring logic *is* the moat.

### The 7 moats for AI startups (Lightcone: Jared Friedman, Diana Hu, Harj Taggar)
1. Process Power (battle-tested integrations at scale)
2. Resource Power (proprietary datasets / exclusive access)
3. Switching Costs (embed in the workflow)
4. Counter-Positioning (per-task pricing vs. per-seat; models incumbents can't copy)
5. Brand & Trust
6. Network Effects (usage -> better data -> better product)
7. Scale & Compounding (own a vertical niche)

Key framing quotes: *"A moat is not a starting point, it's a result"* (Jared Friedman)
and *"You have to build the castle first, then you can dig the moat"* (Harj Taggar).
For pre-PMF, **speed is the only defense.** Implication for a hackathon judge: they
will reward something that's genuinely *useful and embedded in a workflow* over
something that's clever but detached.

### Application layer + vertical agents are where YC sees the value
Lightcone "What Surprised Us Most in 2025" (Dec 22, 2025): the real opportunity is
"moving back to the application layer." Vertical AI agents pitched as potentially
"10X bigger than SaaS." Best-defended example cited: a vertical agent in a regulated
industry that swaps models freely *because it owns proprietary evals*. Also notable:
in the Winter 26 batch, **Anthropic overtook OpenAI as the #1 API** among YC founders
 - a relevant data point given OpenAI/Codex is a sponsor here (we can use either; this
is just context, not a constraint).

The current Summer 2026 RFS list (16 ideas) skews hard-tech, but several are directly
GTM/agent-relevant:

## YC portfolio proof points (the thesis is already funded)

- **Coldreach (YC W23)** - *"Find ready-to-buy leads by monitoring custom signals."*
  Workflow is literally DETECT -> ENRICH -> SCORE -> ACT: "AI agent starts monitoring
  the signal in your ICP market, 24/7," delivers "a list of leads ready to buy today,
  updated daily." Example signals: hiring for AP processors, new Compliance Analyst
  hires, job posts needing "Salesforce API experience." Core line: *"you can't sell to
  someone who doesn't need help, even with the perfect message."* Claims teams waste
  ">85% of time reaching out to the wrong people."
- **Orange Slice (YC S25, the host)** - "agentic sales enrichment spreadsheet";
  "prospect, enrich, qualify, and automate GTM work with AI"; "find companies that
  already want to buy" via web + social signals + firmographics. Has both a web app
  and a builder/Claude-Code install path.
- **Unify, Floworks (AI SDR "Alisha"), Bluebirds, AiSDR, Apten, Roger, Throxy, Item** -   a deep bench of YC GTM/signal/SDR-agent companies.

**Strategic read:** judges have seen 50 "AI SDR" and "buying-signal" demos. The bar is
not "do signal-based selling"; it's a *novel, custom, technically interesting signal*
wired end to end with visible scoring logic (the eval/moat), demonstrated on real data.

## How "YC-flavored" framing helps our pitch

1. **Open with the Tan quote as our north star.** "Custom evals/insights are the moat"
   justifies why we picked a weird, hard-to-buy signal and built custom scoring.
2. **Use YC's own vocabulary:** "hair-on-fire problem," "make something people want,"
   "do things that don't scale," "counter-positioning," "embed in the workflow." This
   reads as native to the judges and host (all YC-adjacent).
3. **Quantify in dollars, bottoms-up** (YC demo rule): "X reps x Y hours saved" or "Z
   net-new ready-to-buy accounts/week." Lead with the number.
4. **Show the working product on a real signal** (criterion #1 + YC "product is the
   proof"); skip slides per both YC and the hackathon rules.
5. **Lean into a moat YC respects:** make the *scoring/eval logic* the visible
   centerpiece - that's the defensible, technically-complex part (criterion #2) and
   the literal YC-defined moat.
6. **Counter-position on pricing/outcome** if relevant: "we score and act on outcomes,
   not seats" echoes the AI-Native Services RFS.

## Idea hooks for the hackathon

- **A genuinely custom signal nobody buys off the shelf.** Pick a weird public source
  (e.g., changes in a target's public API docs/changelog, new MCP/integration listings,
  open-source repo activity, SEC/EDGAR 8-K language, regulatory filings, job-post diff
  over time, podcast/YouTube transcript mentions) and run full DETECT->ENRICH->SCORE->
  ACT. The differentiator vs. Coldreach/Orange Slice is *the signal source* + *visible
  custom eval*.
- **"Eval-as-moat" demo.** Make the scoring model the star: show a transparent, tunable
  eval that ranks accounts by fit + intent, with explanations a rep trusts. This is the
  Tan quote made literal and scores high on technical complexity.
- **Signal -> action loop that closes.** Don't stop at a list; auto-draft the outreach,
  push to CRM, or trigger an agent action, and show the *closed loop* with a measurable
  outcome on real data (DoorDash/"do things that don't scale" ethos, automated).
- **"Company Brain" mini-version for GTM.** Pull fragmented internal signals (CRM notes,
  Slack, support tickets, call transcripts) into one queryable layer that surfaces
  expansion/churn/upsell signals - maps to two active YC RFS (Company Brain, AI OS).
- **Agent-discoverable GTM tooling.** Expose the whole pipeline as an MCP/CLI so an
  agent can run it autonomously - directly answers the "Software for Agents" RFS and is
  technically impressive.
- **Counter-positioned, outcome-priced framing** in the pitch even if the build is a
  tool: score and act on *outcomes*, echoing AI-Native Services.

For RocketRide overlap (the 10% bucket): this maps cleanly onto RocketRide's
agent-node / pipeline model - a DETECT->ENRICH->SCORE->ACT signal pipeline is a
natural RocketRide pipeline, so a win-condition build can double as a RocketRide demo.
